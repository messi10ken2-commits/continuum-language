# Continuous regional panorama maps

Each learning language uses one uninterrupted wide painted landscape across the complete scrollable map. The renderer creates exactly one `painted-panorama`, with full-canvas, nonrepeating image layers. It no longer duplicates a portrait scene per viewport. The station, village café and garden occupy different horizontal regions of one shared river, horizon and terrain.

## Worlds and assets

English uses the established countryside/village style; Spanish uses a Mediterranean coastal town; Portuguese uses a Brazilian tropical coastal neighbourhood and heritage tram; Japanese uses a riverside town, tiled wooden buildings, cherry blossoms and local railway. Region identity is selected by the learning language, independently of interface language.

Twelve new assets in `public/living-map-panorama` provide matched quiet/day/evening panoramas for four languages. The built-in image tool used the approved regional portrait scenes as stylistic references, recomposed each as a single wide landscape with one station on the left, a café in the middle and a distinct garden on the right, then made registered daytime and quiet-state edits. Prompts explicitly prohibited repeated or mirrored panels, seams, collage and UI. Entire images are normalized to 1920×896 WebP; no portrait tiling or side-by-side image stitching is used. SHA256 hashes, versioned URLs and per-world effect coordinates are recorded in the manifest. Earlier immutable assets remain available for older clients.

## Map layout and motion

The world spans at least three viewport widths. The authored chapter sequence is distributed across the available width, with 3–5 chapter nodes per region for current courses. All chapter buttons remain within the canvas. Drag, touch scrolling and zoom explore the same continuous scene; replay/pause controls are mounted at the viewport frame so they stay available at every scroll position and replay does not reset exploration.

Station, café and garden reveal masks are aligned to percentage coordinates in each panorama rather than repeated at every viewport. Four completed nonempty chapters still progressively restore the station (1), open the café (2), grow the garden (3), and crossfade to evening lighting (4). Completion uses each selected language/CEFR level's full learning record and authored pass thresholds. Existing rewards, assessment levels, companion auto-scroll and Premium rules remain unchanged. Replay markers stay scoped by account/language/level. Reduced motion shows a static completed scene.

Validation covers asset bytes/aspect/dimensions, chapter criteria, distinct regional selection and geometry for 8–25 chapters at multiple viewport sizes. Browser checks cover every restoration stage in all four languages, exactly one nonrepeating panorama, left/middle/right exploration, pinned controls, replay without a scroll reset, drag, zoom, last-chapter selection, language switching without progress leakage, reduced motion and narrow-screen bounds.


## Object-only depth motion

Motion unlocks after the existing fourth criterion (four complete, nonempty chapters). Earlier restoration stages reveal their scenery without translating any part of the landscape. At the final stage a stationary, train-free evening plate replaces the scene. Transparent train and gardener sprites use local CSS perspective and `translate3d`/rotation/scale for restrained depth motion; this is a layered painted scene, not a navigable 3D mesh. Independent cloud shapes and light glows animate without moving the sky or terrain. Steam is attached only to the English locomotive. The previous radial train crop, flower-region rotation and moving water overlay are removed. Static foreground masks keep the station edge and English lamp in front of the train. Controls sit at the upper right to keep the first lesson tappable.

Replay remounts the object animations without resetting exploration. Pause freezes all object animations; reduced-motion preference shows the complete scene without animation. Map geometry, chapter criteria, rewards and companion behavior are unchanged.

### Generated assets and prompts

The built-in image-generation tool prepared clean plates and RGBA cutouts from the existing regional evening paintings. Generated transparency is preserved when trimming transparent margins, downsampling and encoding WebP. Existing artwork is retained for earlier clients and restoration phases. Final project paths are `public/living-map-motion/{en,es,pt,ja}-still.webp`, `public/living-map-motion/{en,es,pt,ja}-train.webp`, and `public/living-map-motion/{en,ja}-person.webp`. Spanish and Portuguese use the English gardener sprite at their flower garden. All asset dimensions, placements, hashes and versioned URLs are recorded in `public/living-map-panorama/manifest.json`.

Prompt set (regional subject substitutions in brackets):

- **Clean plate:** “Use case: precise-object-edit. Create a clean background plate for animation from this EXACT panoramic painting. Remove ONLY the entire [regional train] at the left; for English and Japanese also remove ONLY the foreground gardener at the far right. Reconstruct existing empty curved railway tracks, plants and paving naturally behind those objects. All café visitors remain. Lock camera, horizon, composition, all object positions, colours, evening lighting and painting texture. Keep the 1920:896 panorama aspect and registration. No new objects, people, buildings or vehicles. Preserve the complete original image everywhere else. Stationary clean plate, not a new scene.”
- **Train cutout:** “Use case: background-extraction. Extract ONLY the [green steam locomotive and visible carriages / white-and-blue passenger train / yellow heritage tram including pantograph / cream-and-purple passenger train] as one transparent RGBA sprite. Preserve the three-quarter perspective, facing direction, vehicle design, proportions, colours, warm windows and painted style. Frame closely with a small transparent margin, entire visible train within canvas. Reconstruct parts obscured by foreground lamps, fences, pots or station; none of that stationary scenery appears in the cutout. No smoke, track, ground, trees, hills, buildings, people, halo, ellipse, rectangular backdrop or cast shadow. Genuine transparency outside the vehicle silhouette.”
- **Person cutout:** “Use case: background-extraction. Extract ONLY the foreground gardener at the far right into a tightly framed transparent RGBA sprite. Keep the straw hat, light shirt, dark blue work clothes, forward-bending gardening pose, warm sunset light, three-quarter direction and painted style. Entire person including hands and feet; reconstruct tiny occluded parts. No plants, watering can, flowers, paving, chair, scenery, shadow, background or other people. Genuine alpha outside the silhouette. Purpose: subtle independent person animation inside the existing painting.”

Validation includes actual alpha chunks and immutable asset hashes; rendered unlock gating in all four languages; real learner restoration/replay/pause/zoom/selection/reduced-motion checks; and `scripts/qa-map-object-motion.mjs`, which compares two animation frames and requires zero changed scenery pixels outside object bounds.

## Train ghosting during restoration

The original `day.webp` panoramas still contain their painted trains. At phases 1–3 the station reveal mask feathered those vehicles into the quiet background, leaving a translucent carriage even though no motion sprite was mounted. That makes the bug progress-dependent and therefore different between language/CEFR maps.

All three restoration reveals now use a reviewed train-free daytime plate, `restorationDay`, in each theme. The original train-bearing daytime assets remain immutable for older clients but are never referenced by the current renderer. The independent train still appears exactly once at phase 4. Final-stage motion, chapter criteria and progress storage are unchanged.

The built-in image-generation tool edited the four original daytime paintings with this prompt set: “Use case: precise-object-edit. Make a registered train-free restoration background plate from this exact daytime panorama. Remove only the entire regional train and every carriage; for the English locomotive also remove every smoke plume. Remove train reflections, cast shadows, headlight glow, faded edges, detached parts, wheels, couplers and remaining silhouettes. Reconstruct existing empty curved track, platform, railing, plants and scenery naturally. No train may remain, including behind the station, and no ghost or semitransparent vehicle. Preserve all other people, café visitors, gardener, buildings, flowers, furniture, water, mountains, sky, daytime lighting and brush texture. Lock the camera, framing, horizon, positions and 1920:896 aspect ratio. No zoom, reframing, added objects or UI.” Regional subjects were the green steam train (en), white/blue passenger train (es), yellow heritage tram and pantograph (pt), and cream/purple passenger train (ja).

Generated assets are normalized to 1920×896 WebP at `public/living-map-motion/{en,es,pt,ja}-day-still.webp`; the manifest records their hashes and versioned URLs. Asset-integrity tests cover these plates. Rendering regressions use actual chapter histories for all four languages, A1–C1 and phases 0–4, prohibit the old train-bearing backgrounds, and assert zero train sprites before phase 4 and exactly one after unlocking. `scripts/qa-map-restoration.mjs` exercises the same 100 combinations through the learner UI and captures A1/C1 restoration views for visual inspection.
